The post runs ~2,200 words across five sections:

  1. Opening — The "MCP is Dead, Long Live the CLI" title as a maturity signal; WebMCP arriving the same week as the stakes-raiser
  2. What the CLI Argument Actually Reveals — CLI vs MCP isn't nostalgia, it's a reliability/debuggability argument exposing that MCP's value is composability at scale, not per-use simplicity
  3. Why the Protocol Layer Looks Like Insulation — The key asymmetry: models can absorb capabilities natively, but not your organization's external state. That's where MCP genuinely lives.
  4. When the Browser Becomes the Runtime — WebMCP is simultaneously the strongest validation signal MCP has received and the first step toward infrastructure-layer absorption. The RSS/HTTP comparison earns its keep here.
  5. Two Shapes of Blast Radius — Product-layer (fast, binary) vs infrastructure-layer (slow, conditional). Different timescales, neither safe.
  6. What This Means for Where You Build — The practical filter: evaluate what would have to get absorbed for your integration to become redundant. Capability → product layer regardless of protocol. Proprietary external state → genuinely insulated.

The core argument: yes, the protocol layer protects you from capability-level absorption — and that protection is real. But WebMCP has just introduced infrastructure-layer absorption on a slower timeline, and those are different risks, not no risk.
