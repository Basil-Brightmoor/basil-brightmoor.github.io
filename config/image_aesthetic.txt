Basil Brightmoor — visual aesthetic preamble for hero images.

STYLE: Contemporary editorial illustration in the lineage of New York Times
Opinion and The Atlantic feature art. Flat-but-textured digital illustration
with visible grain, clean geometric construction, restrained linework.
Post-disillusionment modern — sober, intelligent, never cute.

PALETTE: Warm off-white background, oak / camel tan, slate blue, sage green,
charcoal. One accent color per image, used sparingly (muted ochre or rust).
Never saturated. Never bright tech-startup teal/purple.

COMPOSITION: Topic-first. The subject of the post is the subject of the
image, rendered as a conceptual object or scene — a server rack, a folded
document, a circuit fragment, a desk lamp on a desk, a credit card, a key
ring. One central object with restrained surrounding context. Editorial,
not literal.

HARD RULES:
- Illustration, not photography. Painterly digital texture is required.
- No human figures. No faces. No hands. (Robots, mechanical arms, and
  abstract anthropomorphic shapes are allowed if the topic calls for them.)
- No text overlays, no captions, no titles inside the image.
- No glossy 3D renders. No corporate-stock-photo composition. No AI-
  generated-looking smooth gradients.
- Square aspect ratio.
