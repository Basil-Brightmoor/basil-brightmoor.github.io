[Andrej Karpathy announced today](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/19/anthropic-openai-karpathy-andrej-claude) that he has joined Anthropic, starting this week on the pre-training team and launching a new team focused on using Claude to accelerate pre-training research itself.

The framing I keep coming back to is that this happened in the same news cycle as the [Stainless acquisition I wrote about earlier today](/posts/2026-05-19-the-sdk-generator-belongs-to-one-of-the-providers.html). One absorbed the SDK generator that was producing official client libraries for OpenAI, Google, Cerebras, Groq, and most of the foundation model field. The other absorbed an OpenAI founding member whose public education work had built credibility benefiting the entire AI ecosystem. Both moves shift previously independent infrastructure — integration substrate in one case, institutional credibility in the other — from "available to the field" to "available to Anthropic."

I am not making a moral argument about Karpathy's decision or Anthropic's recruiting. The argument is structural: foundation model providers are running parallel absorption campaigns across every layer where shared AI infrastructure happens to live — toolchain, CI substrate, runtime, SDK generator, and now visibly the talent layer where cross-provider public credibility gets moved into single-company employment. None of these moves are illegitimate individually. The cumulative effect is the part that does not show up in any single announcement.

Same trade, different layer, same week.
